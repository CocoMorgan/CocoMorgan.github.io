For the Circuit Plushy project, I was able to design and create a salmon-colored, light up plushy using conductive thread and simple circuits for the first time! I was able to make the plushy light up with three LEDs by using a slide switch that is turned on and a button that is pressed.

I started this project by first making a paper prototype in order to plan the circuit layout. Then, I was set to test this paper prototype using alligrator clips, and once it was confirmed that everything worked, I sewed the different componenents into the felt. I will say that the order of this process was slightly different for my case, because I tested the alligator clips after I had sewn everything, instead of the reverse. I have approval from Professor Howley for my project and this change because I had problems with a piece breaking off of my battery. This is also why my sewed components don't light up because the battery is broken, but I've included a photo below using the alligator clips with a non-broken battery that allowed the LEDs to turn on, in order to show that I set everything up corrrectly. Again, I had approval from Professor Howley to do this.

I used a running stitch for the electrical connections (conductive thread) and whip switches to assemble the plushy (non-conductive thread), which gave me more practice in doing neat stitches. Overall, I would say this project taught me a lot of patience, helped me to to better understand circuits in a hands-on way, and gave me more practice with sewing and design.

A general tip I would give my past self for completing this assignment is to have patience and work slowly. First, when I was working with conductive thread, I learned very quickly that it can easily get tangled easily and break, so my advice is to work slowly. Second, I tried to follow a Sparkfun post that said to break off comnponents, which I've now learned will make my life a lot harder in the future - again, this mistake was a reminder to work slowly and reach out to my Professor and/or TAs before making big decisions. Finally, I learned that the entire reason why my LEDs weren't turning on in office hours with Professor Howley that this was because my battery had a gold piece that broke off. Again, this was a reminder to work slowly, have patience, especially because at this point I was feeling really frustrated, and be extremely gentle with the components. So, overall: have patience, stay calm, and move slowly because that will actually save you time in the long-run.
