<ins>Brief Description</ins>: This project simulates a high-security museum display reacting to theft. A felt copy of a Louvre jewelry necklace will glow green under normal conditions. When a black glove hovers over the piece, it casts a shadow detected by a light sensor. Only when the glove both creates this shadow and touches a conductive fabric patch (homemade button) does the system trigger an alarm. Red LEDs will blink and a buzzer will sound.

<ins>NOTE</ins>: I've included two different versions of paper prototypes, where one shows the digital circuiting with the glove and jewelry (harder to read visually) and the other is a more simplified version without the glove and jewelry (which I confirmed in Office Hours with Professor Howley).

<ins>Self-Reflection</ins>: After completing the Paper Prototype assignment, my reflection is that when confused about digital circuits, it's best to consult the class slides and then Professor Howley. The TAs are really helpful with the coding, but don't have as much experience with digital circuiting; so, being able to go to office hours will Professor Howley ended up being really helpful because I was able to double-check that what I was doing was correct, and make some quick fixes. Note that this was not put in the assignment description but was included in the rubric so I'm including a self-reflection for this website post just in case.
